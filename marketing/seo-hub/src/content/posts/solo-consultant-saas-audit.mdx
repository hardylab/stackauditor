# The solo-consultant SaaS audit checklist

A 30-minute pre-audit checklist for fractional operators, solo consultants, and 1–5 person agencies. Run through this before you upload screenshots to [StackAuditor](https://hardylab.github.io/stackauditor/) (or before you open the DIY spreadsheet), and the audit itself takes five.

This is the checklist I wish someone had handed me the first time I tried to audit my own stack. The point is not to make the audit faster — it is to make sure you are auditing the right thing, in the right order, with the right data ready.

## Before you start

1. **Pick your audit window.** Two weeks before the largest renewal. Quarterly is the default cadence; align to your cash-flow calendar.
2. **Pull your last 90 days of card and ACH transactions.** Tag every line item that looks like a SaaS charge. The list is longer than you think.
3. **Open a single spreadsheet.** Eight columns: tool, annual cost, active users last 30 days, active users last 90 days, renewal date, owner, replaces, action.

## Pull the data

4. **For each tagged tool, pull "active users last 90 days" from the admin panel.** Skip this and the audit is just a price list. The 90-day number is the diagnostic.
5. **Mark which tools are on annual vs monthly.** Annual tools with a renewal in the next 90 days get priority. Monthly tools can be cancelled today.
7. **Note which tools were bought by which human.** The owner is the human who can answer "do we still use this" without guessing.

## The 12-step audit

8. **Sort by annual cost descending.** The top 5 tools are 70–80% of your wasted spend, by dollar. Start there.
9. **Flag every tool where active users last 90 days is less than 50% of seat count.** These are dormant-seat candidates.
10. **Flag every tool that has a sibling in the same category.** Two CRMs, three project managers, two password managers. Pick one or none.
11. **Flag every tool on annual contract with a renewal in the next 90 days.** These are the only ones where the audit is urgent.
12. **Pick your action per flagged tool: keep, cut, migrate, renegotiate.** Renegotiate means call the vendor. Cut means cancel before renewal. Migrate means switch tools. Keep means do nothing, but write the reason.
13. **Add up the annualized cost of every tool you flagged as cut.** That number is your ROI.
14. **Run the audit again in 90 days.** The audit is a habit, not a project.

## What to do with the output

The output of the 12-step audit is a one-page spreadsheet. You can hand it to:

- Yourself, 90 days from now, when you forget which tools you cut.
- A client, if they pay you to run ops for them.
- A fractional operations consultant, if you decide to outsource the work.
